# Comparative Analysis of Hair Loss Trends and Its Psychological, Social, and Academic Impact Among Bangladeshi Students

A survey-based research study examining the prevalence, causes, and multidimensional impact of hair loss among university students in Bangladesh, with a focus on mental health, social behavior, and academic performance.

## 📄 Abstract

Hair loss is increasingly prevalent among Bangladeshi students yet is often dismissed as a cosmetic concern. This study investigates its psychological, social, and academic implications through quantitative survey data collected from 200+ students across multiple universities.

## 🎯 Objective

To measure the rate and root causes of hair loss among Bangladeshi students and analyze how it affects their mental health, social relationships, and academic performance.

## 🔬 Methodology

| Component | Details |
|---|---|
| **Data Collection** | Google Form online survey |
| **Participants** | Bangladeshi university students |
| **Sample Size** | 200 respondents |
| **Data Type** | Quantitative and categorical |
| **Variables** | Academic stress, sleep quality, diet, family history, hair product use, hair loss frequency/severity, confidence level, social interaction, academic focus |
| **Statistical Approaches** | Descriptive statistics, correlation analysis, charts, crosstabulation |

## 📊 Key Findings

- **75%+** of respondents reported some form of hair loss
- **60%** experience hair loss daily or frequently
- Main causes identified: stress, environmental factors, and academic pressure
- **40%+** feel stressed or worried about their hair loss regularly
- **35%** reported reduced confidence in class presentations or social interactions
- A notable subset reported avoiding social events or facing bullying
- **22.5%** said hair loss significantly affected academic motivation or performance
- **50%** reported no effect on study focus; **12.5%** said it affects them significantly

### Age of Onset

| Age Group | Frequency | Percentage |
|---|---|---|
| Below 15 | 11 | 5.5% |
| 15–18 | 81 | 40.5% |
| 19–22 | 92 | 46.0% |
| 23+ | 16 | 8.0% |

The majority (86.5%) first noticed hair loss between ages 15–22, corresponding to peak academic years.

## ✅ Recommendations

- Implement mental health support programs in universities
- Raise awareness through seminars and workshops
- Promote healthy lifestyle habits among students
- Provide on-campus dermatological support services

## 📝 Conclusion

Hair loss among students is not merely a medical issue — it intersects with emotional well-being and academic life. Institutions should invest in mental health resources and awareness programs to support affected students.
